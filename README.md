How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?

I interpret user needs by taking note of the user's goals and needs then I identify which features would allow the software to meet those standards. Creating user stories helps because, rather than focusing only on the technical side of development, the user stories detail the user's unique needs from their own point of view. For example, in the SNHU Travel project, a user story explained how a user wanted to be able to receive a list of the top five travel destinations based on their profile. Making this requirement into a user story made it much easier to understand what exactly the user wanted and how we needed to manipulate the software in order to accommodate this. Additionally, user stories can be used to create acceptance criteria and test cases, which helps ensure that the finished program meets user expectations.


How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs initially by viewing and trying to understand the requirements, then breaking the work into smaller, manageable pieces. This course helped me understand that development does not have to mean building the entire project at once. In future development work, I hope to use Agile practices such as user stories, sprint planning and reviews, prioritizing the product backlog, and retrospectives. I also want to make sure there is an open line of communication for regular feedback throughout development so that problems can be identified and corrected before they become larger issues.


What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, completing assigned work in a timely manner, listening to other team members' concerns and ideas, and being willing to help when problems arise. Software development requires different team members to all work together, so it is important to respect a variety of different ideas even if I do not agree with them. I also learned that being a good team member means being accountable for my own work and communicating when I need clarification or assistance or when something may affect the team's progress. In an Agile environment, I would also contribute during Scrum events and be open to feedback so the team can continue to improve.
